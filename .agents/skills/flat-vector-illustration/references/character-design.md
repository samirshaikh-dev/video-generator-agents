# Character Design — Meelte Flat Vector

## Proportions

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Head-to-body ratio | 1:5.5 to 1:6 (slightly stylised, not chibi) |
| Head shape | rounded rectangle or soft oval, jaw softly defined |
| Shoulders | 1.6–1.8 head widths |
| Limbs | tapered cylinders, no muscle definition |
| Hands | simplified mitten or three-finger form, thumb implied |
| Feet | simple rounded wedges, no laces, no tread |

Never chibi (1:3) for business contexts — it reads as a children's brand. Never realistic (1:8) — it reads as stock illustration.

## Face

The face carries the entire emotional load. Keep it minimal:

- **Eyes:** two solid shapes, optional single highlight dot. No iris rings, no lashes unless the character is explicitly female-presenting and it fits the style.
- **Brows:** short single strokes; they do most of the expression work.
- **Nose:** one short stroke or a small soft shadow shape, or omitted in three-quarter view.
- **Mouth:** one shape — a curve for a smile, a small open oval for surprise.
- **Cheeks:** optional soft blush shape at 8% opacity.
- **Never:** teeth, tongue, individual eyelashes, wrinkles, skin texture, nostrils, ear detail.

## Expression table

| Expression | Brows | Eyes | Mouth | Head tilt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm smile | level, slight lift | open, engaged | wide gentle curve | 0–5° |
| Pleased surprise | raised | slightly larger | small open oval | 5–10° |
| Focused | slight inward | narrowed | flat line | level |
| Friendly greeting | raised outer | open | curve with corner lift | 5° |
| Relief / satisfaction | level | softly closed curve | small curve | 0° |

Pick one expression per character. Two characters in one scene should not share the same expression unless the scene is about agreement.

## Hands

Hands are the highest-risk area in generated illustration.

1. Design poses that keep hands simple: holding a flat object, resting on a counter, gesturing open-palm.
2. Avoid: interlocked fingers, gripping thin handles, pointing at small text, holding a phone at an angle.
3. State the hand treatment explicitly: "simplified hands, mitten form, no individual fingers."
4. If a hand is not needed for the story, occlude it behind a prop or the frame edge.

## Poses

- Weight on one leg, not both square — gives life without complexity.
- Shoulders at a slight angle to camera, never fully frontal and never fully profile.
- One clear action per figure. Two actions read as confusion.
- Keep silhouettes readable: the pose should be identifiable as a solid black shape.

## Clothing

Clothing is a flat shape with 2–3 internal division lines maximum (collar, sleeve seam, hem).

- Use brand tokens: purple, orange, lavender, charcoal, white.
- Avoid patterns. If a pattern is needed, use a single large simple motif.
- No logos, no text on garments, no real brand marks.
- Occupational shorthand is allowed only when accurate: apron for a food vendor, not a stethoscope for "business owner".

## Diversity and representation

- Vary age, build, gender presentation, and skin tone across a series.
- Match the campaign audience and market; do not default every character to the same template.
- Skin tones are flat fills from a small warm-neutral set, not gradients.
- Avoid stereotype markers: no "village" shorthand, no exaggerated accents in dress, no poverty cues unless the brief explicitly calls for them.

## Consistency across a series

Characters in a series must share:

1. Head-to-body ratio and head shape family.
2. Outline weight and colour logic.
3. Facial feature construction (same eye and brow vocabulary).
4. Hand treatment.
5. Shading model — one light direction.

Characters may vary in: age, build, clothing colour, hairstyle, pose, expression.

## Failure signals

- Hands look like gloves with five lines — fingers were rendered.
- Faces have rendered noses and lips — detail budget exceeded.
- One character is detailed and the other is simple — inconsistent style.
- Everyone is the same age and build — representation failure.
- Silhouette is unreadable — pose too complex.
