# Message Hierarchy — Reference

## The five layers

A Meelte creative carries at most five pieces of information, in this order of visual weight:

| Layer | Job | Word limit | Visual weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Headline | deliver the primary message | <= 6 | largest type, highest contrast |
| 2. Focal subject | show the message happening | n/a | largest shape, strongest colour contrast |
| 3. Supporting line | add the proof or mechanism | <= 14 | medium type, secondary colour |
| 4. CTA | state the action | <= 4 | contained shape, accent fill |
| 5. Logo | identify the brand | n/a | smallest, footer zone |

If a layer has nothing useful to say, remove it. An empty slot is better than filler.

## Building the hierarchy

1. **Primary message** — complete this sentence: "After seeing this, the audience should believe ___." One clause. If it needs "and", split the campaign.

2. **Proof** — what makes that believable? Only from supplied facts. If nothing is supplied, the supporting line carries the *mechanism* instead ("Publish once, order anytime").

3. **Action** — what should they do? If the answer is "nothing yet", omit the CTA. Awareness creatives do not need one.

## Headline patterns that work for Meelte

| Pattern | Example | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome stated plainly | "Open before you open" | default; safest |
| Owner's words | "My shop, my rules" | community/festival campaigns |
| Contrast | "Less counting, more selling" | replacing a manual process |
| Invitation | "Bring your shop online" | acquisition |
| Time relief | "Close the shop, not the orders" | always-on selling |

Avoid: puns that need explaining, questions that answer themselves, "Introducing…", "Say hello to…", "The future of…".

## Supporting line patterns

- Mechanism: "Publish your catalogue once; customers order ahead."
- Removal of friction: "No app to install, no new hardware."
- Scope: "Works for one shop or five."
- Reassurance: "Your prices, your stock, your control."

The supporting line never repeats the headline's words.

## CTA patterns

Verb + object, specific to the action:

- "Publish your catalogue"
- "Start selling on Meelte"
- "Open your shop"
- "See how it works"

Avoid: "Learn more", "Click here", "Get started" (vague), "Buy now" (wrong for a B2B tool).

## Copy length by format

| Format | Headline | Supporting | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1:1 | 3–5 words ideal | 8–12 words | optional |
| 4:5 | 3–6 words | 10–14 words | recommended |
| 16:9 | 4–6 words | 8–12 words | recommended |
| 9:16 | 2–4 words | 6–10 words | optional |

Longer than ideal means the copy is doing layout's job.

## The three-second test

Show the creative for three seconds, then ask: what is it selling and to whom?

- Both correct: pass.
- Brand correct, offer unclear: headline problem.
- Neither correct: hierarchy problem — usually two competing messages.

## Failure signals

- Supporting line restates the headline — delete it.
- CTA is longer than the headline — inverted hierarchy.
- Headline contains three ideas — no primary message.
- Copy explains the feature but not the benefit — strategist step skipped.
- Every layer is bold — nothing is prioritised.
